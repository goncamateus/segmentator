\section{Next phase}
\label{sec:next}

Five pieces of work, each answering a specific item in §\ref{sec:limitations},
in the order they unblock each other.

\paragraph{A labelled evaluation set, and the numbers that follow it.} The
missing measurement is the one that matters most, and everything else in this
list is easier to justify once it exists. A few hundred labelled frames spanning
the conditions the footage actually contains --- plume present and absent, steady
and gusting, across the lighting the day imposes --- turns ``it visibly separates
the plume'' into a detection rate at a stated false-alarm rate, and turns recipe
tuning from a judgement into an optimisation with a target.

\paragraph{Generalisation across scenes.} A second camera and a second
installation would establish which of the current design assumptions are about
this problem and which are about this footage. The specific question to answer
first is whether the fixed region mask and mean background survive a scene where
the camera moves or the background is not static; if they do not, the adaptive
subtractors already in the registry are the replacements to measure.

\paragraph{Throughput headroom.} \texttt{\BenchDearestStage} is
\BenchDearestShare\% of the frame in the motion recipe, so there is one thing to
attack and its cost is known. Beyond that, frame-level parallelism is the obvious
next factor and the architecture permits it: a single forward pass over
independent frames is close to embarrassingly parallel except where a stage holds
state, and which stages those are is already declared on the stage classes.

\paragraph{Deployment hardening.} Streaming input from a live camera, a
supervised process that restarts rather than exits, output to somewhere other
than the local disk, and an alerting path. This is ordinary engineering rather
than research, and it is deliberately not begun: what it should do depends on
where it runs, and that is a conversation with whoever runs it.

\paragraph{Classical output as bootstrap labels.} The route to a learned model
that does not start with a blank labelling tool. A tuned chain over a season of
footage produces crops, per-object geometry and per-frame scalars; a human
correcting that output is doing a much cheaper job than a human producing it, and
the result is the training set that §\ref{sec:validation}'s missing accuracy
measurement also needs. This is the item that makes the classical pipeline and a
learned model the same project rather than competing ones.
