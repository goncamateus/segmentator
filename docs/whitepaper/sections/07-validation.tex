\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

This section makes three separate claims and supports them separately: that the
operators do what they say, that the engine runs the recipes it ships, and that
it does so fast enough to matter. None of them is a claim about detection
accuracy; §\ref{sec:limitations} says why there is no such claim anywhere in this
document.

\subsection{The suite}

\NumTests{} tests, \TestLOC{} lines of them, run against \RepoLOC{} lines of
engine. The largest group asserts operator output directly --- given this input
array, this operator produces these edge pixels, these contour counts, this many
keypoints, this HOG dimension --- rather than asserting that a run completed. The
editor is covered too, headless, under the offscreen Qt platform.

One test file is worth naming separately. \texttt{tests/test\_examples.py} drives
all \NumExamples{} shipped example configs through the CLI end to end, which
makes it a drift guard rather than a correctness test: if a stage is renamed, a
parameter is removed, or a config comes to reference something the registry no
longer has, the suite fails on the config rather than on a user's run. The
appendix of this paper is generated from the same registry
(Appendix~\ref{sec:catalogue}), so a catalogue entry that does not exist is not a
document error that can survive a build.

\subsection{Synthetic media, and why the figures use it}

Every figure and every measurement below comes from two synthetic scenes: a
plume-like clip and a still with hard geometry and texture patches, both written
by \texttt{docs/whitepaper/synth.py} and both regenerated by \texttt{make}.

The clip is a stand-in with plume-like statistics --- puffs emitted at a fixed
point, each with its own velocity, growing and cooling as they drift, over a
static scene with sensor noise and fixed structures hot enough to be excluded by
a region mask. It is deliberately \emph{not} a physical model of gas dispersion,
and no conclusion in this paper depends on it being one. The still exists because
the structure and texture operators need something to describe: edges, corners
and local binary patterns have nothing to say about a soft-edged drifting cloud,
and a figure showing them failing on one would misrepresent what they are for.

The choice to publish figures made from synthetic scenes rather than field frames
is not only a disclosure decision. A field frame in a document is an assertion a
reader has to take on trust. These figures are the output of the shipped recipes
over media the reader can regenerate, which makes every number in
Table~\ref{tab:throughput} and every panel in Figure~\ref{fig:plume} something
they can reproduce and disagree with.

\subsection{Throughput}

Table~\ref{tab:throughput} measures the \NumExamples{} shipped example configs at
three frame sizes. They are the shipped recipes: the only edits are the source
path, which points at synthetic media, and the removal of the preview windows,
which need a display. Stage lists are untouched.

Timings are end to end and include the sinks --- encoding, CSV and JSON writing
--- because that is what a batch run costs, not what an idealised chain costs.
Each figure is the median of three runs of 900, 300 and 150 frames respectively
(the larger sizes get fewer frames: ms per frame is the comparable quantity, and
$1280\times720$ is twelve times the pixels). The machine is one core of an AMD
Ryzen 5~5600X. The GPU in it is idle, and nothing in the engine uses one.

Three things come out of the table. At field resolution every video recipe runs
faster than the footage arrives: the slowest, \texttt{\BenchSlowestVideo}, at
\BenchSlowestVideoFps~fps against a 15~fps camera.
The cost is concentrated rather than spread: Table~\ref{tab:stagecost} shows
\texttt{\BenchDearestStage} taking \BenchDearestShare\% of the
\texttt{\BenchCostConfig} recipe's frame, so the question ``can this be made
faster'' has one place to look rather than seven. And the scaling is close to
linear in pixels (Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}), which is what a chain of per-pixel and
local-neighbourhood operators should do, and which is what makes the $720$p
numbers predictable rather than surprising.

\iffieldnumbers
The synthetic clip is not flattering itself. On \FieldFrames{} frames of the
original field footage --- $320\times240$, 15~fps, twenty-seven minutes, not
included in this repository and not shown anywhere in this document --- the
\texttt{\FieldSlowest} recipe runs at \FieldSlowestFps~fps against
\FieldSynthetic~fps on the synthetic clip at the same size. The synthetic scene
is marginally the more expensive of the two.
